How it reads a photo. It finds the coin's circle, crops to it, evens out brightness and colour cast, and reduces the face to a 16 × 16 colour fingerprint. That is compared with the same fingerprint of all 10,000 obverses, at five small rotations. Every Cent's patina is different, so the right one stands well clear of the rest.

How far to trust it. Tested on 118 Cents from cents.vision and the Instagram set, each in ten kinds of copy (original, heavy JPEG, 120 px, dark and light screenshots, tight crop, 4° rotation, colour cast with blur, phone-style with JPEG, black background). The right Cent came first in 1,179 of 1,180 trials and second in the other. "Match" was shown 1,098 times and was right every time. With the true Cent removed from the index, "Match" never appeared in 1,180 trials.

What it can't do. Only obverses are indexed, so a reverse will not match. A brand-new photo of a coin, under different light, is a harder problem than a copy of an official image and has not been tested. Images stay in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
