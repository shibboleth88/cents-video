# Surprising stories in the Walls and the Cents — method and first findings

Data: cents.vision snapshot of 2026-10-27 16:33Z (`/mnt/project-files/collectors/snapshots/2026-10-27/`).
Scan: `pipeline/stories.py` (branch `claude/project-thread-k86gy1`). Full output: `stories_2026-10-27.txt` and `.json` beside this file.
Status: leads for the user to react to. Nothing here is approved narration.

## 1. The question

wall_focus.py (PR #18) asks: *which of this Wall's qualities do the fewest other Walls share?*
That finds a Wall's lead among the qualities the site already measures (Sets, coverage, hoards).
The user's ask is wider: **things collectors may not already know.** A collector already sees
every badge and Set on their card. So the scan adds a second axis: **is this shown on cents.vision?**

Every finding carries the same fields, so findings from different lenses can be ranked together:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `bits` | surprise. −log2 of the chance of the finding under a random null (hypergeometric tail, or share of Walls that match). 10 bits ≈ 1 in 1,000. |
| `on_site` | the site already shows it (1/1 badges, Sets). Off-site findings are the "may not know" candidates. |
| `cents` | the Cent numbers that make it true, for checking by eye. |

## 2. The lenses

Collection (Cents only):
- **pairs** — traits that never meet, or meet far more than chance (19 Atlas traits + mint marks; 209 tests).
- **era** — traits whose Cents are much older/newer than the collection.
- **extremes** — oldest Cent of a trait *by a margin*, only Cent of a trait in a mint mark, most traits on one Cent, two Full grades.

Walls (claimed Walls by name; unclaimed Walls only ever as counts, never addresses):
- **share** — a Wall holds a trait far beyond what its size predicts (a focus or taste; selection is the collector's own).
- **grip** — one Wall holds a large share of a whole trait pool.
- **nearmiss** — one Cent short of a Set or of full coverage.
- **smallyear** — Cents from years with ≤4 Cents in all 10,000.
- **pair** — the only collector holding both of two scarce values.
- **holders** — who holds each extreme Cent.

Controls (`--self-test`, all green; each was seen red first: grip and smallyear returned 0 on the first run and the floor caught it):
our Set rules reproduce the site's own counts (Chronicle 53, Full Mint 28, Spectrum 16, Full Contamination 3);
a planted Wall with 1971+1982 is reported "one 1979 short"; a planted Wall holding every Ghost is reported as 33 of 33;
shuffling the Faceless flag across random Cents drops its strong pair findings from 6 to 0 (the lens finds structure, not noise);
every lens returns findings; no wallet address appears anywhere.

Verification of the headline findings used a **second source and code path**: `claimed_walls.json` (the page's own replay, 24 Oct)
and `knowledge/data/cents_by_trait.json` (Sep 2026 export; identical to the 27 Oct metadata, 0 of 10,000 differ). Every number below matched.

## 3. First findings, collection (off-site)

1. **White Drips & Streaks live on desert Cents.** 79 of the 82 are also desert, 16x what chance gives (4.9). The Atlas describes the drips against *obsidian*, but only 51 are obsidian. (The Atlas's desert entry does say "whitish", so check by eye whether the two readings feed each other.)
2. **Ghost Cents are desert Cents.** 26 of the 33 are desert, 13x chance.
3. **Fingerprints are a modern trait.** Only 3 of 224 are dated before 1965 (#370, #3172, #4765; chance gives ~26), and no Wheat Cent has one (chance ~5).
4. **No Cent carries two Full grades.** Each of the 154 Full Cents is Full in one patina only.
5. **#9344 (1974) carries 9 of the 19 traits**, the most on any Cent: faceless, gold, abraded, corroded, orange, obsidian, red, verdigris, Blood contamination. 5,259 Cents (53%) carry none. Held by an unclaimed Wall.
6. **Oldest by a margin.** #9526 (1910, one of the two 1910 Cents) is the oldest Faceless Cent by 34 years, the oldest Desert by 30, the oldest contaminated by 17. #2616 (1912) is the oldest Red by 15 years. #6749 (1927) is the oldest Orange by 17 years. #1264 (1944) is the oldest Gold by 12 years.
7. **Corrosion and gold travel together:** 13 of the 69 Corroded Cents are gold, 27x chance. Corroded + Iridescent: 4 Cents, 29x.
8. **No collector dominates any trait.** No collector holds more than 14% of any trait or contamination pool of 8+ Cents (tightest: Daidatep, 5 of the 35 3D-black). The rarities are spread across the community.
9. Probably by definition, so check the Atlas before using: no Cent is both Lustrous and Abraded (chance ~8) or Lustrous and Striated (~7); 9 of 20 Iridescent Cents are gold (the Iridescent entry names "yellow to gold" hues).

## 5. How to use it for an episode

1. Run `wall_focus.py` (the site-visible lead) and `stories.py --wall <name>` (the off-site leads).
2. Lead with the strongest *off-site* finding when it is ≥ ~10 bits and visually showable; otherwise the wall_focus lead.
3. Recount the chosen finding from a second source before scripting (as in §2), and look at the Cents by eye.
4. Wording: "holds 7 of its 41 Cents in Orange, about twelve times its share" is a count. Never say why: no intention for the collector beyond "holds", and never for Chang. "The only collector" excludes the artist wallet. Unclaimed Walls are never named.
5. Rare sats are left out.

## 6. Limits, and lenses worth adding next

- The metadata sees only what the Trait Council recorded. Visual oddities outside the 19 traits need eye review of the top Cents.
- Movement over time (which Walls grew, Mint Keeper changes) needs more dated snapshots; there are two so far (25 and 27 Oct).
- Next: exact-profile uniqueness (Cents whose full trait set is unique), Cent-number runs, decade-by-trait tables for One Cent context lines.
- 209 pair tests: at 10 bits about 0.2 false findings are expected, so anything under ~10 bits in the pairs lens is weak.
