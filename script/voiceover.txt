A copper penny is worth one cent.
The copper inside it is worth about two and a half.
Rutherford Chang's CENTS begins in that gap.
Over several years he sorted a hundred thousand pennies down to ten thousand pre-1982 coppers.
He photographed and catalogued every one.
Then he melted them into a single block.
Then he inscribed each destroyed penny onto a sat, the smallest divisible unit of Bitcoin.
The block itself filled an entire Bitcoin block — CENTS Block 839969.
He released all ten thousand at one sat each.
Each Cent is five natures at once.
A unit of currency. A quantity of copper. A melted element of a sculpture. A photograph. A Bitcoin inscription.
All of these things, and none of them.
Conceptual art broke from the object in the nineteen sixties, when commodification had hollowed it out.
Digital flows could not hold identity, singularity, provenance or ownership.
They lacked objecthood.
Blockchain gave them discrete boundaries and verifiable histories.
Blockchain is this era's objecthood.
CENTS holds that whole arc in one gesture.
Its medium is the gesture of its continual becoming.
A digital object is simultaneously itself, not itself, and an interpretation of itself.
