Rutherford Chang began CENTS with a simple value discrepancy.
What was one cent to the US Treasury was about two and a half cents as copper at the time he made the piece.
Nothing had been sold for a single cent since before Chang was born, yet the object remained, buoyed into physicality by the force of its context.
The readymade he chose is also perhaps the most reproduced physical artwork on Earth.
More than five hundred billion Lincoln cents were produced, each with the portrait of Lincoln that Victor David Brenner made in 1909.
It is absurd that a nation's currency should dissolve into worthlessness.
It is absurd that a cent should be valued at hundreds or thousands of dollars.
Yet here we are.
Chang brings a kind of black hole humor to his work, laughing at this absurdity with a laughter that resonates within emptiness.
In the system Chang created, every cent is passed through five transformations of value.
One, in circulation, its value defined by the Treasury.
Two, removed from circulation, that value assignment subverted.
Three, archivally photographed, valued for what the image holds.
Four, destroyed, melted into a block of copper, its value made undefinable.
Five, inscribed on Bitcoin, its value continually redefined by decentralized consensus as the objects are traded every day on the open market.
Chang cracks open the worthless one-cent coin and allows it to function as a vessel of indeterminate value and emergent meaning.
CENTS is a becoming, a gesture that unfolds continually through the actions of its participants through time.
