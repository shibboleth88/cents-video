CENTS & digital objecthood — RECORDING SCRIPT
====================================================

35 sentences, 299 words.
At 130-145 words a minute that is about 124-138 seconds of speech, before pauses.

HOW TO RECORD
  One take, straight through. Do not stop to fix a stumble — say the line
  again and carry on; the alignment keeps whatever you actually said, and
  a second attempt inside one take is easier to cut than a second take.
  Leave a breath between sentences. Leave four seconds of silence at the
  end: the credits live there.
  Changing a line as you read is fine. The transcript follows your voice,
  not the page, and anything you add is flagged for review rather than
  overwritten.

  Save as: inputs/vo_raw.wav

----------------------------------------------------

 1.  A copper penny is worth one cent.

 2.  The copper inside it is worth about two and a half.

 3.  Rutherford Chang's CENTS begins in that gap.
      [CENTS: say 'cents' — the PROJECT (capitals are for the reader, not the voice)]
      [Rutherford: RUTH-er-ford]

 4.  Over several years he sorted a hundred thousand pennies down to ten thousand pre-1982 coppers.
      [1982: 'nineteen eighty-two']
      [pre-1982: 'pre nineteen eighty-two']

 5.  He photographed each one and catalogued its date and mintage.

 6.  Then he melted them into a single block.

 7.  Then he inscribed each destroyed penny onto a sat, the smallest divisible unit of Bitcoin.
      [sat: rhymes with 'cat'; a satoshi]

 8.  The block itself became a full-block inscription, CENTS Block 839969, the master object of the project.
      [839969: read as 'eight three nine, nine six nine']

 9.  One inscription, filling an entire block.

10.  He released all ten thousand inscriptions at one sat each, about one hundredth of a cent.

11.  Every Cent passes through five transformations of value.

12.  In circulation, its value defined by the Treasury.

13.  Removed from circulation, that assignment subverted.

14.  Archivally photographed, valued for what the image holds.

15.  Destroyed, its value made undefinable.

16.  Inscribed on Bitcoin, continually redefined by decentralized consensus.
      [decentralized: four syllables, do not rush]

17.  And it arrives as five natures at once.

18.  A unit of currency.

19.  A quantity of copper.

20.  A melted element of a sculpture.

21.  A photograph.

22.  A Bitcoin inscription.

23.  All of these things, and none of them.

24.  Conceptual art broke from the object in the nineteen sixties, when commodification had hollowed it out.

25.  A split opened between materiality and meaning.

26.  Digitization widened it.

27.  Digital flows could not hold identity, singularity, provenance or ownership.

28.  They lacked objecthood.
      [objecthood: OB-ject-hood]

29.  Blockchain was built to give them discrete boundaries and verifiable histories.

30.  Blockchain is this era's objecthood.

31.  CENTS holds that whole arc inside one gesture.

32.  Its medium is not the penny, or the photograph, or the block.

33.  Its medium is the gesture of its continual becoming.

34.  A digital object is simultaneously itself, not itself, and an interpretation of itself.

35.  Its meanings keep emerging, as participants co-create the intersubjective reality of its presence.
      [intersubjective: in-ter-sub-JEC-tive]
