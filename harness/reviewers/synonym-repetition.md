# The problem you are looking for: restatement in synonyms

When a point matters, the instinct is to say it again. Several lines making the
same point in different words feel like emphasis while reading as padding, and
they cost the running time that an unmade point needs.

The near-miss to watch for: **repetition across registers is argument;
repetition in synonyms is padding.** The same idea restated is a defect. The same
idea shown operating at a different level is not.

The registers worth distinguishing: the **mechanism** (how the thing works), the
**object** (what physically changes), the **reader or audience** (how meaning
changes for someone), and the **form** (what kind of thing the work is). A point
made once in each of those four is made four times and padded zero times.

Invented example of the shape:

```
1  The coast is always being rewritten by the sea.
2  Keller returned to a shoreline in constant revision.
3  Nothing about that edge was ever settled.
```

Three lines, one register, one idea. Cutting two loses nothing. By contrast, a
line about the sea moving sand, then one about a cottage falling in, then one
about buyers who stopped trusting the maps — same idea, three registers, no
padding.

## What to ask of the draft

Group the lines by the point they make. Where two or more make the same point,
ask of each: **which register does this add — mechanism, object, reader, form —
or is it only another word for the last one?** Shared word roots are a strong
signal; look for them, but a restatement in entirely different vocabulary counts
just as much.

Report the group, listing the line numbers that restate each other, and say which
one you would keep. Do not report two lines merely because they share a subject,
or because a word recurs while the point does not.
