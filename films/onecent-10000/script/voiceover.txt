Cent #10000. Its number is also how many Cents there are in all.
A penny struck in 1976 with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
Cent #10000 carries two traits, and the rarer is Assassin.
The Cents came with no traits, only a year and a mint mark. In the words of Steven Reiss, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "The community therefore began naming the spontaneous variations. Traits like 'Ghost', 'Faceless', or 'Assassin' were born."
In the Traits Atlas, Assassin is "prominent spherical obsidian, verdigris or contamination striking Abe's head, neck, or torso – the mark of an imagined projectile."
Here it is a single green spot at the back of Lincoln's neck.
42 Cents carry Assassin. Two of them were struck in 1976.
The second trait is Notable Verdigris: in the Atlas, "localised but clearly visible patches or films". On Cent #10000, the spot itself is verdigris.
Cent #1256 was struck the same year, and is close to mint state. Its portrait is unmarked. As money, both meant exactly one cent.
An open signifier.
Steven Reiss again: "Their former economic meaning is unhooked from the object and turned into a vessel for socially agreed value. The penny becomes an open signifier that can point to many signifieds - artistic, collector, historical, crypto."
The portrait is Abraham Lincoln, assassinated in 1865. In the Atlas, the Assassin's "singular placement transforms decay into narrative".
In 1968, long before CENTS, Lucy Lippard and John Chandler wrote, in their essay "The Dematerialization of Art": "What something looks like and what it is about may be complementary but not necessarily (rarely) identical."
To look at, Cent #10000 is a 1976 penny with one green spot. What it means, and what it is worth, the people who collect CENTS keep deciding.
Cent #10000, a great Cent.
