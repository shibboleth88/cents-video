[thoughtfully] [calm] Cent number ten thousand. Its number is also how many Cents there are in all. A penny struck in nineteen seventy-six with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint. Cent number ten thousand carries two traits, and the rarer is Assassin. The Cents came with no traits, only a year and a mint mark. In the words of Steven Rice, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "The community therefore began naming the spontaneous variations. Traits like Ghost, Faceless, or Assassin were born." In the Traits Atlas, Assassin is "prominent spherical obsidian, verdigris or contamination striking Abe's head, neck, or torso, the mark of an imagined projectile." Here it is a single green spot at the back of Lincoln's neck. Forty-two Cents carry Assassin. Two of them were struck in nineteen seventy-six. The second trait is Notable Verdigris: in the Atlas, "localised but clearly visible patches or films". On Cent number ten thousand, the spot itself is verdigris. Cent number twelve fifty-six was struck the same year, and is close to mint state. Its portrait is unmarked. As money, both meant exactly one cent. An open signifier. Steven Rice again: "Their former economic meaning is unhooked from the object and turned into a vessel for socially agreed value. The penny becomes an open signifier that can point to many signifieds, artistic, collector, historical, crypto." The portrait is Abraham Lincoln, assassinated in eighteen sixty-five. In the Atlas, the Assassin's "singular placement transforms decay into narrative". In nineteen sixty-eight, long before CENTS, Lucy Lippard and John Chandler wrote, in their essay "The Dematerialization of Art": "What something looks like and what it is about may be complementary but not necessarily, rarely, identical." To look at, Cent number ten thousand is a nineteen seventy-six penny with one green spot. What it means, and what it is worth, the people who collect CENTS keep deciding. Cent number ten thousand, a great Cent.
