Let's explore CENTS Sets and the traits that constitute them.
Every difference to the canvas of the Lincoln cent is made by the marks of touch and time.
The cataloging of these marks is the result of two years of voting.
Out of this catalog, Sets were identified.
Let's look at them in order of rarity.
Verdigris, obsidian, desert and red are the base patinas: one notable trait from each base patina makes a Patina Set.
One significant trait from each base patina makes a Sig Set.
Gold and orange are the noble patinas: a Patina Set that also includes a gold and an orange is a Spectrum Set.
One full trait from each of the base patinas makes a Fulls Set.
Only 15 can exist, as there are only 15 Full Verdigris Cents.
One full from each base patina, plus a full gold and an orange, makes a Full Spectrum Set.
Only 9 such Sets can exist, as there are only 9 Full Gold Cents.
Contaminations are intricately cataloged, in 12 types: glue, scotch tape, 3D-black, alien secretion, paint, obscure adhesive, dark grime, sea salt, blood, speckles, unknown liquid and special oddities.
A Contamination Set covers any 3 of the 12 types, and a Contamination Hoard covers 6.
A Set that includes all 12 types is a Full Contamination Set, and only 7 can exist, as scotch tape is present on only 7 Cents.
The Chronicle Set consists of years whose meaning is essential to the project.
1971: the year US currency became fiat.
1979: the year the artist Rutherford Chang was born.
1982: the last year that pennies were struck in copper, and the last year of the collection.
The Full Mint Set is determined by mint marks.
An S for San Francisco, a D for Denver and no mint mark, usually Philadelphia, make a Full Mint Set, and only sixty Cents carry the S.
Decades Sets consist of every year of the given decade or decades that the collection holds.
On cents.vision, you can find a Trait Atlas that defines each trait, as well as Collector Walls that show completed Sets, and many more features to explore the project.
