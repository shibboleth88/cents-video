Cent #478. A penny struck in 1963, at Philadelphia.
Under the word TRUST, the copper has darkened. That is called significant obsidian.
At his collar, and around the word GOD, pale, dry films. Notable desert.
Across LIBERTY and down the shoulder, small dark particles lie scattered. A contamination called Speckles.
Compare Cent #3525, also from 1963, whose only trait is Lustrous. The same design, the same year, a different surface.
None of these names came with the Cents.
Traits named by the community. In the words of Steven Reiss, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "Unlike most NFT projects, CENTS offered no preset rarity traits beyond mint year and mint mark. The community therefore began naming the spontaneous variations."
A Trait Council of collectors spent about two years naming, defining and voting. Speckles is one of twelve kinds of contamination they recognised. 95 Cents carry it. Four of them are from 1963.
Value made by consensus. In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "CENTS as an artistic expression of Bitcoin's core ideas": "Just as the project removes the authority of the state in determining a penny's worth, it also removes the authority of a single artist or institution in defining its significance."
