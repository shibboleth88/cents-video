Cent number eighty-four sixty-two. A penny struck in nineteen seventy-three, at Philadelphia.
Beside Lincoln's face, someone has engraved a rabbit. Under it, the word Easter, just above the date.
Its one trait is Vandalized.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council defines it as clear, intentional, human-made deformation or marking of the coin. The Atlas gives this Cent as its first example.
Twenty-six of the ten thousand Cents carry the trait. Number eighty-four sixty-two is the only one from nineteen seventy-three.
Compare Cent number twenty-one twenty-five, also from nineteen seventy-three, whose only trait is Lustrous. The same design, the same year. Only one of them was engraved.
Difference makes meaning. In the words of Steven Rice, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "Coins whose look strays farthest from the norm became the most coveted. Difference now drives value, just as Saussure said: we understand a sign by what it is not."
After it was photographed, this penny was melted, with the rest of the ten thousand, into a single copper block. Its copper is in the block. Its image is inscribed on a satoshi.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Rutherford Chang's Conceptual Collecting": "Can destruction create value, just as accumulation does? If mass production standardizes objects, can time and context reintroduce uniqueness?"
