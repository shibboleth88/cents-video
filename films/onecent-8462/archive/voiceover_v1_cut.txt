Cent #8462. A penny struck in 1973, at Philadelphia.
Beside Lincoln's face, someone has engraved a rabbit. Under it, the word EASTER, just above the date.
Its one trait is Vandalized.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council defines it as clear, intentional, human-made deformation or marking of the coin. The Atlas gives this Cent as its first example.
26 of the 10,000 Cents carry the trait. #8462 is the only one from 1973.
Compare Cent #2125, also from 1973, whose only trait is Lustrous. The same design, the same year. Only one of them was engraved.
Difference makes meaning. In the words of Steven Reiss, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "Coins whose look strays farthest from the norm became the most coveted. Difference now drives value - just as Saussure said: we understand a sign by what it is not."
After it was photographed, this penny was melted, with the rest of the 10,000, into a single copper block. Its copper is in the block. Its image is inscribed on a satoshi.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Rutherford Chang's Conceptual Collecting": "Can destruction create value, just as accumulation does? If mass production standardizes objects, can time and context reintroduce uniqueness?"
