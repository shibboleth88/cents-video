Cent #8462. A penny struck in 1973, at Philadelphia.
Beside Lincoln's face, someone has engraved a rabbit. Under it, the word EASTER, just above the date. Collectors have nicknamed it the Easter penny.
This is an exemplary instance of the Vandalized trait.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council defines Vandalized as clear, intentional, human-made deformation or marking of the coin. This was one of the hardest traits to catalog due to the ambiguity of intentionality. The Atlas gives this Cent as its first example.
26 of the 10,000 Cents carry the trait. Others carry scratches, a drill attempt, paint, or a drawing in fine liner. #8462 is the only one from 1973.
Compare Cent #2125, also from 1973, a Cent close to mint state. The same design, the same year. Only one of them was engraved.
Difference makes meaning. In the words of Steven Reiss, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "Coins whose look strays farthest from the norm became the most coveted. Difference now drives value - just as Saussure said: we understand a sign by what it is not."
After it was photographed, this penny was melted, with the rest of the 10,000, into a single copper block. Its copper is in the block. Its image is inscribed on a satoshi.
The copper was altered twice: once by an unknown engraver, once by melting.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Rutherford Chang's Conceptual Collecting": "Can destruction create value, just as accumulation does? If mass production standardizes objects, can time and context reintroduce uniqueness?"
Cent #8462, a great Cent.
