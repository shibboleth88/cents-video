Cent #6129. A penny struck in 1979, made to carry one image: Lincoln.
Look for him. The edge of the profile survives. The features have worn away.
Compare Cent #25, from 1982, which kept its mint lustre: on it, the portrait is still sharp.
The Trait Council calls this Faceless. 85 of the 10,000 Cents carry it.
Where Liberty was struck, something has settled over the letters: Obscure Adhesive.
Around it, the copper has warmed to gold. On the back, it has darkened to obsidian and bloomed with verdigris.
The mark as index. In the words of Steven Reiss, in his article "Materiality and Signification": "The official icon (Lincoln's head) fades; what matters are the unplanned indices. The penny shifts from being an image to being a time-stamp. Its patina says: I have been around."
Only seven Cents are both Faceless and gold.
A coin collector would call this damage. In CENTS it runs the other way. In the words of David Rees, a collector, in the MAKES CENTS interviews: "The more imperfect the coin, the more history you can see, the more value we give it."
Cent #6129: a 1979 penny, Faceless, with Obscure Adhesive, gold, obsidian and verdigris.
