Cent #6129. A penny struck in 1979, made to carry one image: Lincoln.
Look for him. The edge of the profile survives. The features have worn away.
The Trait Council calls this Faceless. 85 of the 10,000 Cents carry it.
Where Liberty was struck, something has settled over the letters: Obscure Adhesive. Around it, obsidian, verdigris and gold.
The mark as index. "The official icon (Lincoln's head) fades; what matters are the unplanned indices. The penny shifts from being an image to being a time-stamp. Its patina says: I have been around."
Only seven Cents are both Faceless and gold.
A coin collector would call this damage. In CENTS it runs the other way. "The more imperfect the coin, the more history you can see, the more value we give it."
One cent, by law. What it is worth beyond that is still being decided.
