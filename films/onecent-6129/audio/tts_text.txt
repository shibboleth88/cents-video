Cent number sixty-one twenty-nine. A penny struck in nineteen seventy-nine, made to carry one image: Lincoln.
Look for him. The edge of the profile survives. The features have worn away.
Compare Cent number sixteen-oh-five, also from nineteen seventy-nine, which kept its mint lustre: on it, the portrait is still sharp.
This trait is called Faceless. Eighty-five of the ten thousand Cents carry it.
Where Liberty was struck, something has settled over the letters: an Obscure Adhesive contamination. More of it covers the D of God.
Between the two, the copper has warmed to gold. In patches it has darkened to obsidian, and in others it has bloomed with verdigris.
The mark as index.
In the words of Steven Rice, in his article "Materiality and Signification":
"The official icon, Lincoln's head, fades; what matters are the unplanned indices. The penny shifts from being an image to being a time-stamp. Its patina says: I have been around."
Only seven Cents are both Faceless and gold.
A coin collector would mark this penny as valueless because of its damage. In CENTS, the damage is valued.
In the words of collector David Rees:
"The more imperfect the coin, the more history you can see, the more value we give it."
