[1] PROJECT DESCRIPTION — sovrn.art/cents/project-description, dated 2024-03-26
================================================================
10,000 cents on 10,000 sats

CENTS examines value through the lens of the copper penny. After over a century in circulation the lowly denomination has become so commonplace that it is considered a worthless nuisance. Yet a penny from any given year will always be worth exactly 1¢, according to the United States Treasury. Furthermore, those minted in 1982 and before were struck in copper and now have a metal value of approximately 3.1¢. Recognizing these discrepancies in value, Rutherford Chang removed 10,000 copper cents from circulation and archivally documented them.

 

With approximately 288 billion Lincoln pennies in existence, the portrait of Abraham Lincoln on the cent's obverse is the most reproduced piece of art on Earth. The course of history has endowed each of these metal discs with unique characteristics, which are now encoded as digital artifacts. Images of these 10,000 individuals are inscribed as ordinals on 10,000 satoshis, the smallest units of Bitcoin, while the physical coins are smelted and cast into a solid 68.56-pound copper block. 

 

This metal block is rendered as a three-dimensional digital model and in turn inscribed as a 4-megabyte ordinal, comprising the entirety of Bitcoin block #839969. Equivalent to casting a copper ingot so heavy and dense that it is indestructible, inscribing this data on the fundamental structure of the blockchain forms an immutable digital monument. As the traditional penny fades into irrelevance and its production is ultimately terminated, its value can continue to be redefined through this digitally scarce set of 10,000 and the block that encapsulates their transformation.

[2] PHILIP TINARI on CENTS — in On NFTs, ed. Robert Alice, Taschen
    Director, UCCA Center for Contemporary Art. Reproduced on sovrn.art.
================================================================
Rutherford Chang's CENTS exists at the intersection of the analog and the digital, and in line with the late artist's lifelong practice of obsessive collecting. Chang's best-known project, We Buy Wbite Albums, entailed a quixotic, decades-long pursuit to buy and catalog every extant copy from the first pressing of the Beatles' signature release, allowing him to compare the accretions and distortions to the palimpsest-like surfaces of these originally identical objects. He generally exhibited this work in the form of a record shop, turning gallery-goers into a community of crate-diggers. For CENTS, he collected pre-1982 pennies, minted in the years before the US Treasury, recognizing the increased value of copper, changed to a zinc alloy. Having dutifully photographed and cataloged these coins, he melted them down into a 68-pound copper block. The individual cents, as well as a 3D model of the cubic copper ingot, were then minted on the blockchain and continue to be circulated as digital inscriptions.

 

Consistently amused by the divergent valuations assigned by societies and economies to different objects, symbols and uses of time, Chang was particularly interested in how, just as the value of a physical copper penny had long surpassed $0.01, the value of his digital CENTS became permanently indeterminate. A lively online community has since arisen around the work, echoing those that have appeared around his earlier projects. As Chang's first and only work on the blockchain, CENTS connects to his ongoing aesthetic and conceptual concerns, and reflects poeticallv on his own life and career, in which the demands of a role in a family technology business were often in tension with the Palo Alto native's own work as an artist. Ironically and tragically, Chang's obituary ran in the New York Times on the same day as a story announcing a government decision to discontinue the production of new pennies.

UCCA Center for Contemporary Art · Beijing
Rutherford Chang: Hundreds and Thousands
17 January – 12 April 2026
CENTS was exhibited in Hundreds and Thousands at UCCA Beijing, the first institutional and most comprehensive solo exhibition of Rutherford Chang’s work. Surveying two decades of practice, the retrospective was co-curated by UCCA Director Philip Tinari and the artist Aki Sasamoto, and presented in collaboration with the Estate of Rutherford Chang.
Rutherford Chang, CENTS , 2017–2024, installation view Artist Rutherford Chang , 1979–2025 Work CENTS #1–10,000, 2017–2024; CENTS Block 839969, 2024; CENTS book Curators Philip Tinari and Aki Sasamoto Presented with The Estate of Rutherford Chang CENTS at UCCA
CENTS is made from 10,000 American pennies minted before 1982 and valued for their copper content. Chang photographed each coin to record its particular wear and patina, then had the collection pressed into a single 31-kilogram copper cube. The images were inscribed on the Bitcoin blockchain, tying digital renderings of physical currency to virtual systems of value.
At UCCA the collection could be explored on touchscreens or in a book, decades of circulation legible in the abrasions and discolouration of individual coins. With the U.S. Mint having permanently suspended penny production in November 2025, the copper block reads as a monument to the coin and to an economy in transition.
The exhibition set CENTS alongside We Buy White Albums (2006–2025), Game Boy Tetris (2013–2018), Class of 2008 (2008–2012), NBC Nightly News (2004), The Epic (2004–2005) and Portraits by Tone (2005). Together they trace a practice built on accumulation, repetition and the slow revaluation of ordinary things.

1\. Traits Rooted in Reality

 * 
Unlike generative 10K collections with algorithmically assigned traits, CENTS traits are derived from real-world physicality. 

 * 
Each CENT is a high-resolution photograph of a genuine coin, and its traits 
are natural phenomena like wear, patina, and imperfections—etched by time, not code. 

 

2\. Surface as a Trait 

 * 
The surface uniqueness of each CENT is unparalleled. Scratches, 
 
contaminations, patinas, and aging patterns create a distinctive fingerprint, 
 
ensuring no two CENTS are alike. 

 * 
These traits offer a visual and textural narrative, turning each CENT into a

[7] THE CATALOGUE — measured from metadata.json, 2026-10-08
================================================================
The collection holds 10,000 records exactly, contiguous and without
duplicates. Mint years run from 1910 to 1982, 58 distinct years. Mint marks:
8,343 with no mark, 1,597 D, 60 S. The trait codebook holds 19 traits in five
families.
50.20 per cent of the collection was struck before 1976; 25.79 per cent
between 1980 and 1982; 2.91 per cent before 1960.
5,306 cents — 53.06 per cent — carry no catalogued visual trait at all.
867 cents are dated 1982 and none later.
Fifteen years between 1910 and 1982 are absent from the collection. Only the
absence of 1943 is anomalous: P(zero by chance) is 2.75 x 10^-5 against a
local mean of 10.5, where the 1913-1933 gaps run 0.37 to 0.85 and are
explained by small numbers. 1942 holds 2 cents and 1944 holds 22.
In 1943 the United States struck the cent in zinc-coated steel to save copper
for the war.
Cents minted in 1982 and earlier were struck in copper; from 1983 the cent was
made of zinc.
The 19 catalogued traits, by family. Patina: verdigris, obsidian, desert, red,
gold, orange, corroded, iridescent. Wear: abraded, faceless, lustrous,
striated. Motif: assassin, fingerprint, ghost, wheat. Residue: contaminated,
white drips and streaks. Alteration: vandalized.

prices in bitcoin of 0.00366 and 0.00367 BTC. At 100,000,000 satoshis to the

five orders of magnitude above the release price.
What this does and does not support: 198 of the 10,000 were listed for sale at

not a valuation of the whole set. Any claim must say "listed" and must carry

[10] EPOCH CONVENTION FOR THIS FILM
================================================================
The film speaks from its own present. The sources do not: each was written or
measured at its own moment and several use bare deictics - "now", "currently",
"still", "today" - which resolve to the SOURCE'S date, never to the film's.

The rule: a source deictic must be converted to an absolute date on the way
into narration. The project description is dated March 2024 and is
contemporaneous with the work, so its "now have a metal value of approximately
3.1 cents" becomes "in March 2024 that copper was worth about three cents".

Three moments the narration must keep distinct:
  THEN  2017-2024, the condition Chang acted in. The March 2024 project
        description governs here: 3.1 cents of copper, 288 billion in existence.
  SINCE February 2025 (the order, and Chang's death) to November 2025 (the last
        cent struck).
  NOW   2026. The catalogue as measured 2026-10-08; the market as observed
        2026-10-06; the UCCA retrospective, 17 January - 12 April 2026.

A figure from one moment must not be given the tense of another. Adding "still"
to a 2024 count asserts a 2026 measurement that nobody made.
