[thoughtfully] [calm] Cent number ninety-three forty-four. A penny struck in nineteen seventy-four with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint. It carries nine of the nineteen traits in the CENTS Traits Atlas, more than any other Cent. No Cent carries eight. Three carry seven. Faceless. Corroded. Orange. Significant Verdigris, Obsidian and Red: each a major visual actor, short of Full. Notable Gold. Notable Abraded: chunky marks where metal is missing. And Blood: a residue named for how it looks, found on seventeen Cents. Beside Cent number eight fifty-one, struck the same year and close to mint state, every one of those marks stands out. No one chose these marks. Chang catalogued each penny by its year and mint mark. Of his work in general, Chang said in twenty seventeen: "I'm not making aesthetic choices during the process. I don't know how to. I don't trust my own aesthetic choice." In the words of Sol LeWitt, in nineteen sixty-seven: "The serial artist does not attempt to produce a beautiful or mysterious object but functions as a clerk cataloguing the results of the premise." Through LeWitt's lens, the premise is ten thousand copper pennies, and Cent number ninety-three forty-four is one of its results, photographed and logged like every other Cent, whatever time had done to it. In the words of Boris Groys, in his book On the New: "a particularly powerful aura surrounds precisely those works in which the highest possible cultural aspirations are combined with the most profane, insignificant, valueless things." CENTS carried the humblest of coins onto Bitcoin and into the galleries of U C C A Beijing. A Cent's worth is no longer set by its metal or its face. The nine traits of Cent number ninety-three forty-four were named after the penny itself was melted, by collectors and the Trait Council. Its value is still being redefined, again and again, by the people taking part. Cent number ninety-three forty-four, a great Cent.
