## 2. Concept and quotes

- **Primary concept: C21 "Permanence and decay"** (time family). Concept base: "A thing destined to corrode is held still. The Cent keeps its corrosion forever, frozen at the moment it was photographed, while the coin itself is gone." Its "show" note: "Active corrosion caught mid-process". The Traits Atlas gives this Cent exactly that: red "marks a transitional oxidation phase between bright copper and darker patinas". Not used by any earlier episode.
- **Value tie: C04 "Value made by consensus"**, spoken in line 15, set up by line 8 ("As money, the two were worth the same: one cent").
- **Art-literature quotes: Walter Benjamin, 1936** (QUOTE_BANK D11 and D10, Zohn translation, *Illuminations*, pp. 220–21).
  - D11: "…the changes which it may have suffered in physical condition over the years as well as the various changes in its ownership."
  - D10: "The presence of the original is the prerequisite to the concept of authenticity."
  - The reading is the one in `art-historical-context.md` §2.7, marked there as ours: CENTS keeps both halves of the history Benjamin names (physical condition in the photograph, ownership on the ledger) while removing the original he made their prerequisite. The film says "Through Benjamin's lens", and Benjamin is never presented as writing about pennies or CENTS (bank flag: About CENTS? No).
- **Traits Atlas lines** (v1.1, `films/traits/script/atlas/red.txt` and `verdigris.txt`, credited "Traits Atlas, CENTS Trait Council") say what each grade means, as the brief requires.

## 3. Script v0.1 (recommended, A)

About **360 spoken words** (numbers spelled out). At eee's measured 2.5 to 2.9 words a second that is about 2:05 to 2:25 of narration, so about **2:20 to 2:40** with the spin, the closing turn and the credits. That is past 2:00, so option B (§6) is there if you want it shorter. Numbers appear on screen as they are spoken.

1. Cent #8820. A penny struck in 1972 with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
2. Two patinas share its face.
3. Significant Red: in the Traits Atlas, red that is "a major visual actor", strong across several zones.
4. 183 Cents carry it.
5. And Notable Verdigris: "localised but clearly visible patches or films". On Cent #8820 the green gathers in the lettering and along the rim, with grey-green patches on Lincoln's face.
6. 29 Cents carry exactly this pair, and nothing else.
7. Both are oxidation of copper. In the Atlas, red "marks a transitional oxidation phase between bright copper and darker patinas".
8. Beside Cent #8653, struck the same year and close to mint state, you can see how far Cent #8820 had moved from bright copper. As money, the two were worth the same: one cent.
9. Permanence and decay. *(concept card)*
10. Chang photographed Cent #8820, as he did all 10,000, partway through that process. Its image was inscribed on a satoshi, and the penny was melted into CENTS Block Copper. The image holds it at that phase for good.
11. Walter Benjamin, in his 1936 essay "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction", wrote that the history of a work of art includes "the changes which it may have suffered in physical condition over the years as well as the various changes in its ownership." *(quote card)*
12. A penny is mechanically reproduced from the start, one of billions. Yet time gave Cent #8820 a history of its own.
13. Through Benjamin's lens, CENTS keeps both halves of that history: the photograph keeps the changes in physical condition, and Bitcoin's ledger records each transfer of the satoshi that carries its image.
14. But Benjamin also wrote: "The presence of the original is the prerequisite to the concept of authenticity." The penny itself is gone. *(quote card)*
15. As a Cent, its worth is no longer set by the state or by its copper. It is set and reset by the people who trade it, discuss it and read its traits.
16. Cent #8820, a great Cent. *(closing spin card, one turn)*

Spoken text for the voice spells numbers out ("eighty-eight twenty", "nineteen seventy-two", "one hundred and eighty-three", "eighty-six fifty-three", "nineteen thirty-six").

## 5. Checks run

**Quotes.** `check_quotes.py --quote`, on the card text exactly:
- D11 with its leading ellipsis: FOUND (verified excerpts 2026-11-04).
- D10: FOUND.
- Controls: "over the decades" for "over the years", and "the idea of authenticity" for "the concept of authenticity": both MISSING.

**Atlas lines** (not in the checker's corpus, so checked against the repo files with `grep -F`): "a major visual actor, with uneven but strong red toning across multiple zones", "Red Patina marks a transitional oxidation phase between bright copper and darker patinas.", "Localised but clearly visible patches or films", "formed by long-term oxidation of copper", "it often pools in low-relief areas and along lettering": all FOUND. Controls "oxidation stage", "short-term oxidation", "high-relief areas": all MISSING. The card writes "localised" in lower case mid-sentence; the Atlas capitalises it at the start of its sentence.

**Counts.** 183, 1,493, 74, 29, 339 and the template's profile were counted from the raw export and agree with the index. Floor: Full Red counted 71, matching the figure in the data README, so the counter is reading the file.

**Relational passes** (video-concept-relations skill):
- *Referents.* "its face" (2) is #8820. "it" (4) is Significant Red. Line 5 names #8820 by number because it follows a 183-Cent scroll, where "here" could point at the pool. "the two" (8) are #8653 and #8820. "that process" (10) is the oxidation of line 7, kept alive by "moved from bright copper" in 8. "that history" (13) is the history in Benjamin's quote and line 12. "its worth" (15) follows "As a Cent", so it can't be read as the melted penny's worth.
- *Order.* Line 10 says Chang photographed #8820 "as he did all 10,000", so the sequence "transitional phase → photographed" can't read as a reason for choosing or photographing this coin. No words of Chang's appear anywhere near Benjamin, so nothing suggests he drew on him. Line 10 joins the inscription and the melt with "and", not "then", because the brief doesn't settle their order in time.
- *Terms.* satoshi, inscription, image and penny stay apart: "its image was inscribed on a satoshi", "the penny was melted", "each transfer of the satoshi that carries its image". The block shot carries only the melt line.
- *Shape.* Opens on the Cent; Benjamin's "the penny itself is gone" is a hinge, not the end; the last spoken idea is worth being set and reset by people, then the Cent's name. Nothing closes. "For good" (10) is the image's permanence, not an ending of the work.
- *Repetition.* "Value" runs in three registers: the state's one cent (8), Benjamin's authenticity (13–14), people's consensus (15). "Change" repeats on purpose in 13, mapping Benjamin's own two terms.
- *Superlatives.* None. "Exactly this pair, and nothing else" is the counted 29.

## 6. Other ways to do it

- **B, shorter (about 315 spoken words, about 2:05 to 2:20 total):** cut lines 12 and 14. Benjamin then gives only the two halves of history, and the film goes from line 13 straight to the value line. It loses the twist that CENTS kept the history without the original.
- **C, a community opening:** add before line 2 "When the collector DOPEMIND asked what to add next, Daidatep's list included Cent #8820." (+16 words, about 6 s.)
- **Not used, and why:**
  - Groys, *In the Flow* (E114–E116), "Digital metadata creates an aura without an object": over a Cent it sounds like the "NFTs restore aura" line that the art-history doc (§2.7) says to avoid, and #9344 leaned on Groys already.
  - Drobnick (B39), "the patina stores the history of the object through surface and texture": apt, but about *We Buy White Albums*, and the blacktongue character episode just used his patina quote.
  - Lippard and Chandler (D37), "What something looks like and what it is about may be complementary but not necessarily (rarely) identical.": could replace line 12 if you'd rather a 1968 voice there.
- **A lead, not verified:** the sentence after D11 in Benjamin's essay is about traces that only chemical or physical analysis can reveal. It would fit the Atlas's chemistry closely, but the bank holds only D10–D12 and I couldn't reach a copy of the Zohn text from here, so it is not used.
