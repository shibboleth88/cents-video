## 2. Script v0.2, with shibboleth's edits, in their order

About **282 spoken words** (numbers spelled out): about 1:37 to 1:53 of narration at eee's 2.5 to 2.9 words a second, so about **1:50 to 2:10** with the spin, the closing turn and the credits. Well under 3:00.

1. Cent #8820. A penny struck in 1972 with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
2. Two patinas share its face.
3. Significant Red: in the Traits Atlas, red that is "a major visual actor", strong across several zones.
4. 183 Cents carry it.
5. And Notable Verdigris: "localised but clearly visible patches or films". On Cent #8820 the green gathers in the lettering and along the rim, with grey-green patches on Lincoln's face like daubs of paint laid by time.
6. 29 Cents carry exactly this pair, and nothing else.
7. Both are oxidation of copper. In the Atlas, red "marks a transitional oxidation phase between bright copper and darker patinas".
8. Beside Cent #8653, struck the same year and close to mint state, you can see how far Cent #8820 had moved from bright copper. As money, the two were worth the same: one cent.
9. Permanence and decay. *(concept card)*
10. Walter Benjamin, in his 1936 essay "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction", wrote that the history of a work of art includes "the changes which it may have suffered in physical condition over the years as well as the various changes in its ownership." *(quote card)*
11. A penny is mechanically reproduced from the start, one of billions. Yet time gave Cent #8820 a history of its own.
12. Through Benjamin's lens, CENTS keeps both halves of that history: the photograph keeps the changes in physical condition, and Bitcoin's ledger records each transfer of the satoshi that carries its image.
13. But Benjamin also wrote: "The presence of the original is the prerequisite to the concept of authenticity." The penny itself is gone. *(quote card)*
14. Cent #8820, a great Cent. *(closing spin card, one turn)*

## 3. How the shorter script reads in order (video-concept-relations passes)

**Referents.** Every survivor still points at something in the piece, or at something the audience knows, which is shibboleth's call for the cut line 10.
- "the photograph" (12) is now the first mention of Chang's photograph. The words lean on what the audience knows; the picture can carry it (§5).
- "The penny itself is gone" (13) no longer has "melted into CENTS Block Copper" behind it. Same: the picture of the block carries it.
- Line 8's "As money, the two were worth the same: one cent" lost its payoff in line 15, but it still has a job: it sets up line 11, alike as money and different by time.
- "Permanence" on the concept card (9) used to be paid off at once by "The image holds it at that phase for good". Now it is paid off by "the photograph keeps the changes in physical condition" (12). Decay comes before the card (5 to 8), permanence after it.
- Line 5's new image says time laid the patches, not Chang, so it implies no intention. It also matches the Atlas, which says verdigris is "valued for painterly variation".

**Order.** No adjacency suggests a motive of Chang's. "Through Benjamin's lens" still marks the Benjamin reading as ours.

**Shape: the one problem.** In this order the last spoken idea is "The penny itself is gone", then the Cent's name. The film now ends on an absence, which frames as finished a work the project says has no ending. It is also close to the closing on the penny's discontinuation that CLAUDE.md rules out. In v0.1 that sentence was a hinge, because line 15 came after it.

**Value.** With line 15 cut, nothing says who sets a Cent's worth now. Value still runs in three places: the state's one cent (8), Benjamin's authenticity (13), and ownership changing hands on the ledger (12). That follows from the cut and is recorded here, not argued.

**Repetition.** "History" appears three times in three registers: Benjamin's general claim (10), this Cent's own (11), what CENTS keeps (12). "Keeps" twice in line 12 is deliberate (what CENTS keeps, what the photograph keeps).

**Superlatives.** None new.

## 4. Recommended: the same lines, with 12 and 13 swapped

No words added back, none changed. Lines 1 to 11 as in §2, then:

12. But Benjamin also wrote: "The presence of the original is the prerequisite to the concept of authenticity." The penny itself is gone. *(quote card)*
13. Through Benjamin's lens, CENTS keeps both halves of that history: the photograph keeps the changes in physical condition, and Bitcoin's ledger records each transfer of the satoshi that carries its image.
14. Cent #8820, a great Cent.

What the swap does:
- "The penny itself is gone" becomes the hinge again, and the film ends on the ledger recording each transfer, which goes on. That is open, as CENTS is.
- The concept card's second word, permanence, is the last thing said before the name ("the photograph keeps").
- "But" still follows a claim it can answer: line 11's "a history of its own". Lines 11 and 12 then turn twice in a row ("Yet time gave…", "But Benjamin also wrote…"): reproduced, yet with a history, but authenticity needs the original. Each turn answers the one before, so I kept both; it is worth hearing in the take.
- "That history" (13) still finds line 11's "a history of its own", two sentences back, and the line names both halves itself.
- One risk, checked: after the original is gone, "CENTS keeps both halves of that history" could be heard as CENTS answering Benjamin and restoring authenticity, the "NFTs restore aura" reading the art-history doc (§2.7) warns against. It doesn't say that: it claims the history is kept, never authenticity, and there is no "yet" or "so" joining the two lines. If shibboleth hears it as a rebuttal anyway, that is a reason to keep their order.
