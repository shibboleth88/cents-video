[thoughtfully] [calm] Cent number eighty-eight twenty. A penny struck in nineteen seventy-two with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint. Two patinas share its face. Significant Red: in the Traits Atlas, red that is "a major visual actor", strong across several zones. One hundred and eighty-three Cents carry it. And Notable Verdigris: "localised but clearly visible patches or films". On Cent number eighty-eight twenty the green gathers in the lettering and along the rim, with grey-green patches on Lincoln's face like daubs of paint laid by time. Twenty-nine Cents carry exactly this pair, and nothing else. Both are oxidation of copper. In the Atlas, red "marks a transitional oxidation phase between bright copper and darker patinas". Beside Cent number eighty-six fifty-three, struck the same year and close to mint state, you can see how far Cent number eighty-eight twenty had moved from bright copper. As money, the two were worth the same: one cent. Permanence and decay. Walter Benyameen, in his nineteen thirty-six essay "The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction", wrote that the history of a work of art includes "the changes which it may have suffered in physical condition over the years as well as the various changes in its ownership." A penny is mechanically reproduced from the start, one of billions. Yet time gave Cent number eighty-eight twenty a history of its own. But Benyameen also wrote: "The presence of the original is the prerequisite to the concept of authenticity." The penny itself is gone. Through Benyameen's lens, CENTS keeps both halves of that history: the photograph keeps the changes in physical condition, and Bitcoin's ledger records each transfer of the satoshi that carries its image. Cent number eighty-eight twenty, a great Cent.
