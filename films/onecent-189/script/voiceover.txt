Cent #189.
Cards, sneakers, watches and coins are graded and traded like securities, and a grade rewards an object for how little has happened to it.
The collectors of CENTS value what happened to each coin.
In a conversation in her 2022 book Proof of Work, Rhea Myers quotes Barbara Kruger: "Money is an information-destroying medium." A circulated copper cent is the exception. Its wear and patina record where it has been.
In the image of Cent #189, that record is red, a trait called Full Red.
In the Traits Atlas, red patina is "a red to reddish-brown surface film resulting from oxidative conversion of copper," and Full Red means red is the dominant surface condition, generally over about seventy percent of the coin.
Seventy-one Cents are Full Red. Cent #189 is the only one dated 1960.
Pale, sandy films of Notable Desert lie on Lincoln's hair, cheek and coat: clearly visible, but local, up to about a fifth of the surface.
Compare Cent #189 with Cent #9794, also a 1960 with a D mark, and close to mint state.
As money, any cent was as good as any other. As a Cent, none is. A Cent's worth is set by the people who keep, exchange, catalog and compare it, and set again each time.
Cent #189, a great Cent.
