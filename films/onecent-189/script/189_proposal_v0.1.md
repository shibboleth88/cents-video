# One Cent #189: proposal v0.1, baseline format (script for review, not voiced)

2026-11-26. First draft for shibboleth's approval. Nothing is voiced, exported or rendered.

## 1. The Cent

- **Cent #189**, inscription 66001226. A 1960 penny with a D mark (Denver), read on the obverse and in the trait export.
- **Traits:** Full Red and Notable Desert. No contamination.
- **Not a 1/1 Trait Combo.** Nine Cents share exactly this profile (#189, #313, #1771, #2308, #2586, #3168, #6491, #8580, #8815), so the script makes no uniqueness claim about the combination.
- **Rarest first.** Full Red (71 Cents) is rarer than the year (115 Cents dated 1960), so the traits carry the episode, not the year. The opening does not give the year and mint.
- **One count worth saying.** Of the 71 Full Red Cents, #189 is the only one dated 1960. Counted twice from the September 2026 export (the derived index and the raw flags). The same count gives non-empty results for other years (1964: 6, 1974: 7), so the counter works. Only two other Full Reds are older (1912 and 1957).
- **Hi-res obverse:** `front.webp` here (copied from collector-wall/blacktongue-character/hires/189.webp).

## 2. The point, and the way in

**Baseline point** (art text v0.6.1, "Now"): "a grade rewards an object for how little has happened to it; the collectors of CENTS value what happened to each coin."

**Concept:** C26, "Coin collecting turned over" (concept card). Its family is sign, so the value tie is **C04, "Value made by consensus"**, carried by the closing line from the baseline's "Value" section. (C26's caution notes #6129 used it as a tie; here it is the main concept.)

**Why this Cent.** The word *red* means opposite things in the two systems:
- In coin grading, a copper coin is "Red (RD)" when it "displays full original color". It is the color the coin kept.
  *Correction (02:05Z):* the designation applies only to Mint State or Proof copper coins ("Color Designations that accompany the numeric grade of a Mint State or Proof copper coin"); this bullet and line 3 dropped that limit.
- In the Traits Atlas, red patina is "a red to reddish-brown surface film resulting from oxidative conversion of copper". It is something that happened to the coin.

#189 is Full Red, so one word on this Cent carries the whole turn from grading to CENTS.

## 3. Script v0.1

Discussion and quotes first, then the traits, as in #1867 v0.8.

1. Cent #189. *(same opening as #1867: play button, "look closer" sticker, one 3DCENTS turn)*
2. Cards, sneakers, watches and coins are graded and traded like securities, and a grade rewards an object for how little has happened to it. *(concept card: COIN COLLECTING TURNED OVER)*
3. ~~In coin grading, a copper coin is called red when it keeps its full original color.~~
   *Correction (2026-11-26 02:05Z, after shibboleth asked "is that true?"):* too broad. The source limits color designations to "a Mint State or Proof copper coin", and Red (RD) is a designation added to the grade, not the grade. Proposed: "In coin grading, an uncirculated copper coin that still shows its original mint color is designated Red." This holds whether the threshold is Littleton's "full" or the 95% that search summaries attribute to PCGS (not opened, so not relied on). Not yet in `voice_v0.1.txt`; it goes into v0.2 with your other answers.
4. The collectors of CENTS value what happened to each coin.
5. Barbara Kruger's line, as Rhea Myers quotes it: "Money is an information-destroying medium." A circulated copper cent is the exception. Its wear and patina record where it has been. *(lit quote card, credited "Barbara Kruger, quoted by Rhea Myers")*
6. In the image of Cent #189, that record is red, a trait called Full Red. *(back to the whole obverse, slow push-in)*
7. ~~In the Traits Atlas, red patina is "a red to reddish-brown surface film resulting from oxidative conversion of copper," and Full Red means it covers more than about seventy percent of the coin.~~
   *Correction (2026-11-26, found by re-reading every paraphrase after the line 3 error):* the Atlas says Full Red is "the dominant surface condition", "generally > ~70% coverage"; the line dropped "generally" and turned a guide into a rule. Proposed: "…and Full Red means red is the dominant surface condition, generally over about seventy percent of the coin." The other paraphrases (lines 2, 4, 5, 10, 12) keep their source's qualifiers or narrow them.
8. The grader's red is what a coin has kept. This red is what happened to it.
9. Seventy-one Cents are Full Red. Cent #189 is the only one dated 1960. *(scroll of all 71, #189 last, spotlit)*
10. Pale, sandy films of Notable Desert lie on Lincoln's hair, cheek and coat: clearly visible, but local, up to about a fifth of the surface. *(zooms)*
11. Compare Cent #189 with Cent #9794, also a 1960 with a D mark, and close to mint state. *(dissolve)*
12. As money, any cent was as good as any other. As a Cent, none is. A Cent's worth is set by the people who keep, exchange, catalog and compare it, and set again each time.
13. Cent #189, a great Cent. *(same ending: closing card, one turn)*
14. Find out more about the project on cents.gallery, and explore the traits on cents.vision. *(end card, reused from #1867)*

## 6. Choices to check

- **Template #9794.** No 1960 Cent has Lustrous, so no Lustrous template exists for the year. #9794 (1960 D, no visual trait) is the cleanest of the 23 untraited 1960 D Cents with 800 px photos (`189_template_candidates_1960D_untraited.jpg`, and `189_vs_templates_9794_4130.jpg` beside #189). "Close to mint state" is my reading of the photo, not a trait.
- **No phorkish.** The baseline's collector line ("Funny that in real life I like the shiny pennies but with CENTS I want the dirty old ones") fits this point exactly, but the word budget took one quote, and Kruger is the published one the quote-bank rule asks for. It can replace Kruger if you prefer the collector's voice.
- **No Chang lines.** The episode is about collectors and graders; Chang is in the credits and the opening card only.
- **The word "red".** The film says the two reds differ. It does not say the Trait Council chose the name in reply to graders; the Atlas gives no such reason, and neither does the script.

## 7. Relational notes (video-concept-relations passes)

- **Referents.** "that record" (6) is the record of line 5. "The grader's red" (8) calls back line 3, still in the piece. "it" in line 7 is red patina. Line 11 names both Cents, because the line before ends on "the surface".
- **Order.** Grading (2–3), then collectors (4), then Kruger (5): the baseline has the same adjacency in "The public", so "collectors value it because it keeps a record" is the baseline's own reading. No sentence involves Chang, so no order can give him a motive.
- **Shot pairs.** No Cent is shown under the grading lines (3, 8), so no Cent is said to grade red. #9794 appears only under "close to mint state".
- **Registers of "what happened".** It appears three times, each in a different register: the grade (2), the collectors (4), this coin's surface (8).
- **Shape.** It opens on the grading world and ends on value "set again each time", then the Cent and where to look next. Nothing closes on a loss or an ending.
