================================================================

[3] BRETTON WOODS — Federal Reserve History, "Creation of the Bretton Woods
    System". EXCERPT-VIA-SUMMARY.
================================================================
In July 1944, delegates of 44 nations met at Bretton Woods. Member currencies
were pegged to the dollar, and the dollar was fixed to gold at $35 an ounce.

================================================================
[4] THE 1964 COIN SHORTAGE AND THE COINAGE ACT OF 1965 — Wikipedia, "Coinage
    Act of 1965", citing the 1964-65 House Committee on Government Operations
    reports "Coin Shortage" and the 1965 Banking and Currency committee
    reports. EXCERPT-VIA-SUMMARY. Date freeze also in CoinWeek, "The date
    freeze, with mintages for 1964- and 1965-dated coins".
================================================================
Banks and their business customers were retaining coins, fearing shortages.
Many of the silver ones were being taken out of circulation by people
believing the price of that metal would rise. Congress allowed the Mint to
continue to strike 1964-dated pieces into 1965. The act, enacted July 23,
1965, eliminated silver from the circulating dime and quarter.

[5] SILVER IN 1964 — USGS Data Series 140, Silver statistics (last modified
    1 September 2023), unit value column. Arithmetic ours, 2026-10-24.
================================================================
1964 unit value: $41,500 per metric ton = about $1.29 per troy ounce.
A 90% silver dime or quarter holds 0.7234 troy ounces of silver per dollar of
face value, so in 1964 its silver was worth about 93% of its face value.
1967 unit value: $49,800 per ton = about $1.55 an ounce, above face value.
CORRECTION 2026-10-24: script v1 said silver was worth more than the coins
in 1964. It was not; the narration now says "nearly".

[8] 1982 — NGC, "The Transitional Cents of 1982". EXCERPT-VIA-SUMMARY.
    USGS Data Series 140, Copper statistics (2017 update). Arithmetic ours.
================================================================
The double-digit inflation of the late 1970s and early '80s again drove up
the price of copper, and the cent's composition was changed. Coining of the
zinc cents commenced on January 7, 1982 at the West Point Mint; brass cent
production at Denver ended on October 21, 1982. Authority: Public Law 93-441
(October 11, 1974).
A 3.11 g cent at 95% copper holds 2.95 g of copper. At the USGS 1982 unit
value of $1,610 per ton that is about 0.48 cents of copper; at the 1980 high
of $2,230 per ton, about 0.66 cents.
CORRECTION 2026-10-24: script v1 said the copper in a cent was worth more
than a cent in 1982. It was not; the narration now says copper had grown too
costly for the cent.

[11] EPOCH CONVENTION
================================================================
The year lines speak in the historical present: each describes its own year,
which is its epoch. The film's own present is 2026. A source deictic (now,
still, currently, today) resolves to the source's date and is converted to an
absolute date on the way into narration.
