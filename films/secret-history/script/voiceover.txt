Let's explore the stories behind the mint years in CENTS by Rutherford Chang.

Each date marks a moment when what money meant was being changed.

1943 is missing. That year copper went to the war, and the cent was struck in steel.

1944. At Bretton Woods, forty-four nations tie their currencies to the dollar, and the dollar to gold, at thirty-five dollars an ounce.

1964. The silver in a dime or a quarter is worth nearly its face value. People hoard change, coins run short, and the Mint keeps stamping 1964 into the next year.

Then dimes and quarters lose their silver.

1971. The dollar can no longer be exchanged for gold. US currency becomes fiat. Material is detached from value, and the effects reach far beyond currency alone into humanity's fundamental relationship to objects.

1979. Inflation runs in double digits. Rutherford Chang is born.

1982. Copper has grown too costly for the cent, and partway through the year the Mint switches to zinc. The collection stops there.

In the Block, the dates are gone: 1910 and 1982 are the same indeterminate mass.

As inscriptions, the value and the meaning of individual years become indeterminate, continually redefined by the perceptions and consensus of participants.
