Although the obverse portrait remains unchanged, these early CENTS carry the weight of another era – the tactile memory of a design born in the early 20th century. Their presence within the collection is exceedingly rare, accounting for only about 2.08% of all CENTS, and they hold an emblematic prestige among collectors. See also: *CENTS: Reflections on Mint Years and Their Long-Term Significance* Definition: Any CENT minted between 1910 and 1958 that retains the Wheat Ears design on its reverse qualifies for this trait. The category is purely chronological, yet symbolically significant: it bridges the transition from pre-modern coinage to the mid-century American industrial aesthetic. Historical Context: The change of reverse design in 1959 – from the agricultural Wheat motif to the architectural Lincoln Memorial – marks a cultural and visual shift in how national value was represented. In this sense, the *Wheat* CENTS function as material witnesses to a changing conception of economy, labor, and nationhood.
