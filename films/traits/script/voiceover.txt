The CENTS traits were catalogued by the Trait Council, a group of collectors.
Over two years, they reviewed the full set of ten thousand Cents several times, through numerous rounds of voting.
Some traits were assigned or denied only after long debate and contested votes.
Each trait has a definition and a minimum threshold, published in the Traits Atlas on cents.vision.
Counts are as of September 2026.
Patina.
Four base patinas are graded in three degrees.
Notable: clearly visible, but local.
Significant: a major presence on the face.
Full: the patina dominates the coin.
Verdigris: green to teal, often pooling in low relief and along the lettering. Fifteen Cents are full verdigris.
Obsidian: dark brown to black. Thirty-eight are full.
Desert: white, sand and ochre, dry and matte. Twenty-one are full.
Red: scarlet to russet, between bright copper and the darker patinas. Seventy-one are full.
Gold: honey gold to pale amber, graded only notable or full. Nine are full.
Orange: tangerine to amber, given only to clear standouts. There are one hundred and forty-one.
Iridescent: a copper surface overlaid with gold, violet and blue. Twenty.
Corroded: oxidation that has gone into the metal, with crusts, pits and blooms. Sixty-nine.
Reflectivity and texture.
Lustrous: original mint shine, with Lincoln bright against a matte field. One hundred and fifty.
Striated: parallel lines crossing the face, made by metal flow during striking. Four hundred and ninety-eight.
Wear.
Abraded: copper missing from gouges and chunky marks, not fine scratches. Notable or heavily abraded. Forty-four are heavily abraded.
Faceless: Lincoln's features lost, through wear, corrosion or residue. Eighty-five.
Residue.
Fingerprint: the ridges of a fingerprint, clear enough to read at once. Two hundred and twenty-four.
White drips and streaks: pale runs, usually across dark patina. Eighty-two.
Contaminated: foreign matter added after minting, in twelve types. Eight hundred and ninety-five Cents in all.
The type names describe what can be seen, not a chemical test.
Obscure adhesive: thick, cloudy deposits. One hundred and fifty.
Paint: a colored layer. One hundred and forty-five.
Dark grime: dirt and oil embedded in the surface. One hundred and twenty-seven.
Unknown liquid: dried pools with tide-line edges. One hundred and nine.
Speckles: small particles scattered across the face. Ninety-five.
Alien secretion: translucent, greenish films. Seventy-nine.
Glue: hardened amber films. Fifty-one.
Sea salt: crystal blooms, bluish to white. Forty-nine.
Special oddities. Forty-four.
3D-black: raised, tar-like black deposits. Thirty-five.
Blood: reddish-brown splashes that look like blood. The name makes no claim that they are. Seventeen.
Scotch tape: rectangles where tape covered the metal. Seven.
Motif.
Ghost: a full or near-full patina that separates Lincoln from the field. Thirty-three.
Assassin: one round mark on Lincoln's head, neck or body. Forty-two.
Wheat: every Cent struck from 1910 to 1958, with wheat ears on the reverse. Two hundred and eight.
Alteration.
Vandalized: marks that show deliberate human intent, such as scratching, drilling or paint. Twenty-six.
About half of all Cents carry no visual trait at all: a clean record of their year.
