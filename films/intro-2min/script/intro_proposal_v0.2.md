## Script (326 words written, 337 spoken)

CENTS, by Rutherford Chang, is a work of its medium. Chang inscribed images of ten thousand copper cents on Bitcoin, and the work shows what happens to money when its record passes from a state to a public network that anyone can read and no one runs. Released at one satoshi each, the inscriptions moved each cent's value from a figure set by the state into an ongoing process, set and set again by the people who keep, read and exchange each Cent.

It began in 2017, when Chang said: "I'm going to start hoarding pennies." The penny, he said, is "the lowest common denominator, it's like junk, it's like nothing." He had, he said, "no idea" what he would make of them.

A copper cent struck before 1983 holds more than a cent's worth of copper. Hoarding them, he said, was like "buying 1.7 cents of copper for one cent and it's still worth one cent." Chang's statement for the work begins from such "discrepancies in value."

He took ten thousand copper cents out of circulation and photographed every coin. Coins made to be identical turned out to differ, each marked by its own wear, toning and color.

He had the coins melted and cast into one block, *CENTS Block Copper*. In March 2024 he inscribed the image of each obverse, one image to one satoshi, the smallest unit of bitcoin, and in April the block's model, which fills a whole block of the chain: *CENTS Block 839969*. His statement calls it "an immutable digital monument."

The coin, its image, its inscription, the copper and the model: each is a cent in its own way.

Collectors preferred the damaged to the clean. A council of collectors reviewed and voted on all ten thousand Cents three times, and agreed on nineteen traits.

Chang's statement ends: "its value can continue to be redefined through this digitally scarce set of 10,000 and the block that encapsulates their transformation."
