Cent number twelve sixty-four. A penny struck in nineteen forty-four with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
Twenty-two of the ten thousand Cents are dated nineteen forty-four. None is dated nineteen forty-three, the year pennies were struck in steel.
It is a Wheat Club year, and a year of the nineteen-forties Set, which no Wall had completed as of October twenty twenty-six.
This Cent is the oldest Gold Cent in the collection, by twelve years.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council describes gold patina as a luminous surface toning, suspended between metal and light.
This one is Notable Gold. The other grade, Full, is a continuous golden sheen over more than about seventy percent of the surface.
Sixty-nine Cents carry gold. Every other one was struck in nineteen fifty-six or later.
Over Lincoln's head lies Significant Obsidian: in the Atlas's words, a major visual actor with strong darkening, but not yet fully dominant.
Near the rim, a fleck of paint marks it as Contaminated.
Compare Cent number five forty-three, also from nineteen forty-four, with no visual trait. The same design, the same year. Only one of them turned gold.
Time-based generative art. In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Thoughts about the unique nature of CENTS traits and their significance": "Each CENT is a high-resolution photograph of a genuine coin, and its traits are natural phenomena like wear, patina, and imperfections, etched by time, not code."
Years that carry meaning. In the same essay: "coins from significant historical years become highly coveted, adding a layer of historical storytelling to the project."
Cent number twelve sixty-four, a great Cent.
