The story of the value discrepancy at the core of CENTS is told in the traits of Cent #1264. The starting point for Rutherford Chang, the fact that the copper in one penny was worth about 3 times as much as a penny, has roots and implications that are activated by the system that he created with CENTS.
In July 1944, at Bretton Woods, 44 nations tied their currencies to the US dollar, and the dollar to gold, at 35 dollars an ounce. Gold was the measure of money.
On August 15, 1971, the United States stopped exchanging dollars for gold. Within two years the Bretton Woods system had collapsed, and currencies floated against each other. US currency became fiat: worth what it is trusted to be worth.
Dislocating the global reserve currency from any material substance had wide-ranging effects, not only on wealth distribution and social equity, but on materiality itself.
These effects continue to bear influence to this day, in economics as well as art.
In the words of Adina Glickstein, in her article "Never Midcurve" for Spike Magazine: "Post-Bretton Woods as much as post-internet, hasn't contemporary art become, if not exactly a commercial product, the financial instrument par excellence?"
It is in the strange tension between 20th century conceptual art and 21st century post-internet art, between absence of an object and digital objecthood, between value discrepancy and speculation, that CENTS resides.
Among collectors, coins from years that are significant to the project's concept have acquired a special resonance. 22 of the 10,000 Cents are dated 1944. None is dated 1943, the year pennies were struck in steel.
1944 also makes this a Wheat Cent, one of 208: from 1909 to 1958, two ears of wheat framed the back of the penny.
This Cent is the oldest Gold Cent in the collection, by 12 years.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council describes gold patina as a luminous surface toning, suspended between metal and light.
This one is Notable Gold. The other grade, Full, is a continuous golden sheen over more than about 70 percent of the surface.
69 Cents carry gold. Every other one was struck in 1956 or later.
Over Lincoln's head lies Significant Obsidian: in the Atlas's words, a major visual actor with strong darkening, but not yet fully dominant. The unique form of the obsidian on this Cent is what gives it its nickname: the Alien Cent.
Near the rim, a fleck of paint marks it as Contaminated.
Compare Cent #543, also from 1944, with no visual trait. Two very different histories within one history.
Struck in the year the dollar was tied to gold, and bearing the marks of all that's come between.
Cent #1264, a great Cent.
