Cent number twelve sixty-four. A penny struck in nineteen forty-four with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
Twenty-two of the ten thousand Cents are dated nineteen forty-four. None is dated nineteen forty-three, the year pennies were struck in steel.
Coins from significant years have acquired a special resonance.
In July nineteen forty-four, at Bretton Woods, forty-four nations tied their currencies to the U.S. dollar, and the dollar to gold.
In nineteen seventy-one that tie was cut. The dollar could no longer be exchanged for gold, and U.S. currency became fiat: worth what it is trusted to be worth. CENTS keeps that question open: what a cent is worth, and who decides.
Nineteen forty-four also makes this a Wheat Cent, one of two hundred and eight: from nineteen oh nine to nineteen fifty-eight, two ears of wheat framed the back of the penny.
This Cent is the oldest Gold Cent in the collection, by twelve years.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council describes gold patina as a luminous surface toning, suspended between metal and light.
This one is Notable Gold. The other grade, Full, is a continuous golden sheen over more than about seventy percent of the surface.
Sixty-nine Cents carry gold. Every other one was struck in nineteen fifty-six or later.
Over Lincoln's head lies Significant Obsidian: in the Atlas's words, a major visual actor with strong darkening, but not yet fully dominant. The unique form of the obsidian on this Cent is what gives it its nickname: the Alien Cent.
Near the rim, a fleck of paint marks it as Contaminated.
Compare Cent number five forty-three, also from nineteen forty-four, with no visual trait. Only one of them turned gold.
Struck in the year the dollar was tied to gold, and now toned the colour of gold: a coincidence no one planned.
