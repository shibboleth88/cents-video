Cent #1264. A penny struck in 1944 with no mint mark, generally indicating the Philadelphia mint.
According to the United States Treasury, a cent is worth exactly one cent. But the older cents were struck in copper, and in March 2024 the copper in one was worth about 3.1 cents. CENTS begins with that discrepancy. How it came about runs through the year this Cent was struck.
In July 1944, at Bretton Woods, 44 nations tied their currencies to the US dollar, and the dollar to gold, at 35 dollars an ounce. Gold was the measure of money. This Cent has since turned gold itself: Notable Gold.
On 15 August 1971, the United States stopped exchanging dollars for gold. Within two years the Bretton Woods system had collapsed, and currencies floated against each other. US currency became fiat: worth what it is trusted to be worth.
In the years that followed, prices rose, copper among them. By 1973 the copper in a cent was worth almost a cent, and the Mint tested a cent made of aluminum. In 1982 it stopped striking the cent in copper and switched to zinc. By 2006 a cent cost more than a cent to make, the copper in the older ones was worth more than their face, and the Mint made melting them illegal. Scavengers kept pulling copper cents out of circulation for their metal. Chang was fascinated by them.
Art had been letting go of its material too. From 1959 to 1962, Yves Klein sold zones of empty space for gold. When a buyer burned the receipt, Klein threw half the gold into the Seine. Lucy Lippard chronicled the six years from 1966 to 1972 as the dematerialization of the art object.
At the same time, art objects became a place to keep value. In 1974, as inflation ran high, the British Rail Pension Fund began buying art.
CENTS holds both movements. Its copper was melted into a block of about 31 kilograms. Its 10,000 images were inscribed on Bitcoin, where no treasury sets their value, only the people who hold them. Each Cent is a unit of currency, a quantity of copper, a melted element of a sculpture, a photograph and a Bitcoin inscription: all of these things and none of them. The question stays open: what a cent is worth, and who decides.
Coins from significant years have acquired a special resonance. 22 of the 10,000 Cents are dated 1944. None is dated 1943, the year pennies were struck in steel.
1944 also makes this a Wheat Cent, one of 208: from 1909 to 1958, two ears of wheat framed the back of the penny.
This Cent is the oldest Gold Cent in the collection, by 12 years.
The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council describes gold patina as a luminous surface toning, suspended between metal and light.
This one is Notable Gold. The other grade, Full, is a continuous golden sheen over more than about 70 percent of the surface.
69 Cents carry gold. Every other one was struck in 1956 or later.
Over Lincoln's head lies Significant Obsidian: in the Atlas's words, a major visual actor with strong darkening, but not yet fully dominant. The unique form of the obsidian on this Cent is what gives it its nickname: the Alien Cent.
Near the rim, a fleck of paint marks it as Contaminated.
Compare Cent #543, also from 1944, with no visual trait. Only one of them turned gold.
Struck in the year the dollar was tied to gold, and now toned the colour of gold: a coincidence no one planned.
