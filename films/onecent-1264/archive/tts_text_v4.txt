[thoughtfully] [calm] The story of the value discrepancy at the core of CENTS is told in the traits of Cent number twelve sixty-four. The starting point for Rutherford Chang, the fact that the copper in one penny was worth about three times as much as a penny, has roots and implications that are activated by the system that he created with CENTS. In July nineteen forty-four, at Bretton Woods, forty-four nations tied their currencies to the U.S. dollar, and the dollar to gold, at thirty-five dollars an ounce. Gold was the measure of money. On August fifteenth, nineteen seventy-one, the United States stopped exchanging dollars for gold. Within two years the Bretton Woods system had collapsed, and currencies floated against each other. U.S. currency became fiat: worth what it is trusted to be worth. Dislocating the global reserve currency from any material substance had wide-ranging effects, not only on wealth distribution and social equity, but on materiality itself. These effects continue to bear influence to this day, in economics as well as art. In the words of Adina Glickstein, in her article "Never Midcurve" for Spike Magazine: "Post-Bretton Woods as much as post-internet, hasn't contemporary art become, if not exactly a commercial product, the financial instrument par excellence?" It is in the strange tension between twentieth century conceptual art and twenty-first century post-internet art, between absence of an object and digital objecthood, between value discrepancy and speculation, that CENTS resides. Among collectors, coins from years that are significant to the project's concept have acquired a special resonance. Twenty-two of the ten thousand Cents are dated nineteen forty-four. None is dated nineteen forty-three, the year pennies were struck in steel. Nineteen forty-four also makes this a Wheat Cent, one of two hundred and eight: from nineteen oh nine to nineteen fifty-eight, two ears of wheat framed the back of the penny. This Cent is the oldest Gold Cent in the collection, by twelve years. The Traits Atlas of the CENTS Trait Council describes gold patina as a luminous surface toning, suspended between metal and light. This one is Notable Gold. Sixty-nine Cents carry gold. Every other one was struck in nineteen fifty-six or later. Over Lincoln's head lies Significant Obsidian: in the Atlas's words, a major visual actor with strong darkening, but not yet fully dominant. The unique form of the obsidian on this Cent is what gives it its nickname: the Alien Cent. Near the rim, a fleck of paint marks it as Contaminated. Compare Cent number five forty-three, also from nineteen forty-four, with no visual trait. Two very different histories within one history. Struck in the year the dollar was tied to gold, and bearing the marks of all that's come between. Cent number twelve sixty-four, a great Cent.
