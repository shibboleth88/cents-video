In the Numismatists Discord, DOPEMIND asked: "What should I add to expand the set? Wondering about the direction for this collection."
As of November 2026, DOPEMIND's Wall on cents.vision holds 12 Cents, from 1963 to 1981.
centoshi replied: "You already know my answer." DOPEMIND wrote back: "1971."
centoshi holds 16 Cents from 1971, more than any other collector.
Of 1971, Martin Lehnen wrote: "It was the year the United States abandoned the gold standard, severing the connection between currency and tangible value."
On November 9, 2026, three of the 362 Cents from 1971 were listed for sale.
Number 2779 is a Ghost Cent with full desert patina: pale toning that dominates the coin and separates Lincoln from the field. Only 13 Cents are both.
Number 8556, a 1971 from Denver, is contaminated, with significant verdigris: a major presence on the face.
And number 5298 carries no visual trait: a clean record of its year.
Any one of them would add the 1971 Club badge to DOPEMIND's Wall.
Daidatep suggested six more, and added: "I could go on and on."
On the same day, all six were listed for sale as well.
Number 8657 is from 1965. Only 90 Cents are from 1965, fewer than any other year of the 1960s, and none of them carries a mint mark.
It has a notable desert patina: clearly visible, but local.
Number 8820, from 1972, carries two patinas: verdigris along the rim, and significant red across the face. 183 Cents have significant red.
On DOPEMIND's Wall, it would complete a Patina Set: all four base patinas.
Number 6451, from 1980, has significant obsidian: dark toning that pushes the copper into shadow. 220 Cents have it.
Number 6875, from 1967, is one of 44 heavily abraded Cents. In the Traits Atlas, heavy abrasion "dominates the read of the coin."
Number 6782 is a 1969 from Denver, with obsidian and significant verdigris. It would add the 1969 Club badge.
And number 5888 is a 1956 from Denver, with notable verdigris. Only 28 Cents are from 1956, 18 of them from Denver. It would be the Wall's first wheat penny.
