On the twelfth of November 2025, the United States Mint struck its last cent for circulation.
A year on, the cent is still legal tender. But by July 2026, 20 states had passed laws letting shops round a cash total to the nearest five cents. In September, Congress passed the Common Cents Act, which would allow the same across the country.
The face value of the cent is being rounded away.
That same September, copper traded higher in London than it ever had before.
A cent struck before the switch to zinc in 1982 is 95% copper. At that peak, the metal in one was worth more than four cents.
The coin says one. For most of 20 years, its copper has said more.
That gap, between what the state decrees and what the metal holds, is where CENTS begins.
Rutherford Chang removed 10,000 copper cents from circulation, and photographed each one, front and back.
He melted them into a single block of copper, 31 kilograms.
In March 2024, the image of each front was inscribed on its own satoshi.
A Cent is a unit of currency, a quantity of copper, part of a sculpture, a photograph, and a Bitcoin inscription. Each is valued by a different measure.
And the measures keep moving. Over two years of voting, collectors named the marks that circulation left on them: verdigris, obsidian, fingerprints, ghost.
The cent is leaving the till. In CENTS, what a cent is worth is still being decided.
