Ten thousand copper cents, photographed, melted into a block, and inscribed on Bitcoin.
So where is the work?
Not in the coins, which were melted, nor in any one image, nor in the block.
Its medium is the gesture of its continual becoming, rather than any of its visible manifestations.
A becoming does not arrive finished.
It unfolds continually, through the actions of its participants, in time.
The cents left the mint identical, and decades of hands, pockets and air marked each one differently.
So the piece is a kind of time-based generative art.
In March 2024, Chang released the ten thousand inscriptions at one sat each, into indeterminate, continual revaluation.
Then the participants began to read the set.
Over two years of voting, the Trait Council named what circulation had made: verdigris, obsidian, desert, ghost, fingerprints.
It defined Sets, ways of seeing the ten thousand as one structure.
This is how a canon forms: words and phrases accrue significance as readers return to them.
Each reading redefines what a Cent is, and what it is worth.
Rutherford Chang died in January 2025.
That November, the last penny for general circulation was struck.
The work went on.
In January 2026, CENTS was shown at UCCA Beijing, in the first institutional solo exhibition of Chang's work.
Even loss is not an ending here.
Some Cents have been swept, by wallets that do not track inscriptions, into vast shared holdings of bitcoin.
They cannot be traded, but they are not destroyed.
As of 24 October 2026, thirty-one have entered the Void, and none has been recovered.
Its meanings continue to emerge as participants co-create the intersubjective reality of its presence.
