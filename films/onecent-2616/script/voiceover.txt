Cent #2616. A penny struck in 1912, at Philadelphia.
The only Cent from 1912 in the collection. One of 12 1/1 Years, and one of only two before 1919.
Scarcity written by survival.
Among the 10,000 Cents, 21% are from before 1970. Before 1960, under 3%. Before 1950, under one. Before 1940, just 26 Cents.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Reflections on Mint Years":
"This data reveals how quickly scarcity accelerates the further back in time you go."
And of the 1/1 Years: "These CENTS are extraordinary anomalies with absolute scarcity: …1912, and 1911 stand as singular relics."
Its surface has turned red. Full Red. In the Traits Atlas, full means red dominates the surface: a clearly red cent at first glance.
Across Lincoln's head and shoulder, grey-green films. Significant Verdigris. Significant means a major visual actor, but not yet fully dominant.
71 Cents are Full Red. This is the oldest. The next is from 1957.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Lustrous CENTS", transformation is "neither singular nor uniform, but a process that unfolds at different speeds, in different ways."
Coin collecting turned over.
In the words of collector Dagmar:
"Unlike traditional coin collectors, who prefer old coins in pristine condition, we “degens” value the symbolic decay of these fiat coins."
