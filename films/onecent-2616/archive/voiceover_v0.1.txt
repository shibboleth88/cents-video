Cent #2616. A penny struck in 1912, at Philadelphia.
It is the only Cent from 1912 in the collection.
Its surface has turned red. Full Red. In the Traits Atlas, full means red is the dominant surface condition, continuous across the coin, a clearly red cent at first glance.
Across Lincoln's head and down to his shoulder, grey-green films. Significant Verdigris. Significant means verdigris is a major visual actor, with strong presence, but not yet fully dominant.
71 Cents are Full Red. This is the oldest of them. The next is from 1957.
Compare Cent #9540, from 1911, whose only trait is Notable Obsidian. The same design, one year apart, a different surface.
Scarcity written by survival.
Among the 10,000 Cents, the older the year, the fewer there are. 208 are wheat cents, from 1958 or earlier. 12 years appear only once.
In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his essay "Reflections on Mint Years": "These CENTS are extraordinary anomalies with absolute scarcity: 1954, 1949, 1939, 1935, 1934, 1930, 1929, 1928, 1926, 1919, 1912, and 1911 stand as singular relics."
Coin collecting turned over.
In the words of collector Dagmar: "Unlike traditional coin collectors, who prefer old coins in pristine condition, we “degens” value the symbolic decay of these fiat coins."
