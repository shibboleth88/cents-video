Cent #1867.
Circulation leaves traces.
In the words of Emily Watlington, writing on Rutherford Chang in Art in America: "Circulation is another of Chang's key themes: how do things change as they move through the world?"
Rhea Myers, in her book Proof of Work, quotes the artist Barbara Kruger: "Money is an information-destroying medium."
A circulated copper cent is the exception. Three traits record where Cent #1867 has been: two patinas and a contamination.
A dark band of Significant Obsidian crosses the upper left, over the motto and Lincoln's head.
Green patches of Significant Verdigris gather on his cheek, behind his head and at his collar.
The third trait came from outside: Contaminated, Alien Secretion. In the Traits Atlas, contamination is "matter imposed upon copper", and Alien Secretion names "unidentifiable translucent or greenish films".
79 Cents carry Alien Secretion, and 70 of them carry verdigris too.
Cent #4421, dated 1964 like Cent #1867, is close to mint state. Its portrait is clean and bright.
The Trait Council named every kind of residue by reading the surface, without testing it. In the Atlas's words: "The subtrait names are therefore not claims of chemistry, but field names – tools for seeing."
Names like these were put forward and agreed on together. In the words of Martin Lehnen, in his article "CENTS as an artistic expression of Bitcoin's core ideas": "the decentralized formation of meaning and value consensus around CENTS functions as an artwork in itself."
In March 2024, Chang inscribed the image of Cent #1867 on a satoshi, and the physical coin was melted into CENTS Block Copper.
Released at one satoshi, the Cent's value moved from a figure set by the state into an ongoing process, set again each time someone reads it, keeps it or lets it go.
Cent #1867, a great Cent.
