## 3. What could be new to someone who knows CENTS well

1. **Two records of one circulation.** The patina is the coin's record up to the day it was photographed. From the inscription on, the ledger keeps the record. #1867's second record is short so far: three transfers, then one wallet (as of November 2026).
2. **Bitcoin talked about coins before CENTS had any.**
   - The founding paper defines its coin as a chain of signatures.
   - The Ordinals handbook says ordinal theory "imbues satoshis with numismatic value". It explains a digital artifact by the example of a rare coin.
   - In both, the coin is a figure of speech. CENTS put the images of ten thousand real coins there.
   - The handbook quote is from the crypto text. ordinals.com blocks automated fetches, so checking it needs an upload or a saved copy.
3. **The double move.** Ordinals made one interchangeable unit, the satoshi, tellable apart. CENTS did the same to another, the cent, and set the one on the other. A collector said it on the second day of the release: "Fungible pennies become non-fungible digital artifacts" (thedaoistpunk).
4. **The problem is older than coins.** Clay tokens sealed in envelopes, from about 3700 BC, could not be checked without breaking them (Schmandt-Besserat, in the crypto text). Bitcoin made a record anyone can check, holding a piece of information that can be in only one place, and every Cent depends on that.
5. **A thing became a process.** Dodd's critique of Bitcoin, that it treats money as a thing, is turned around by a work that put a thing on it.
6. **Grading inverted.** A grade pays for an absence of history, while the traits name the history.
