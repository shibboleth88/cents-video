## 1. The point, and why this Cent leads into it

**Baseline point:** "The metal and the stamp, joined in every physical coin, now exist apart: the copper in one block, the faces in ten thousand inscriptions." (The work; it rests on the penny section's "the coin's face and its metal had come apart".)

**Concept:** C02, "The gap between assigned and material value". It is in the value family, so value carries the episode.

**The way in.**
1. On #1867, both patinas lie over what the state stamped. The obsidian band crosses the motto and Lincoln's head, and the verdigris sits on the portrait.
2. They are the copper's own chemistry. The third trait came from outside, which sharpens the contrast.
3. So the surface already shows the two things a coin is made of, the metal and the stamp, at odds.
4. From there the film goes to the gap in value between them, to CENTS taking them apart, and to what became of value.

## 2. Script v0.7

1. Cent #1867. *(same opening: play button, "look closer" sticker, one 3DCENTS turn)*
2. A dark band of Significant Obsidian crosses the upper left, over the motto and Lincoln's head.
3. Green patches of Significant Verdigris gather on his cheek, behind his head and at his collar.
4. A third trait came from outside: Contaminated, Alien Secretion. In the Traits Atlas, Alien Secretion names "unidentifiable translucent or greenish films".
5. Compare it to Cent #4421, also dated 1964 and close to mint state. *(dissolve)*
6. On Cent #1867, the copper's own chemistry spreads over the stamp of the state.
7. Every physical coin joins two things: metal and stamp. *(concept card: "Metal and stamp")*
8. In a copper cent, the coin's face and its metal had come apart. Hoarding such coins, Chang said in 2017, was like "buying 1.7 cents of copper for one cent and it's still worth one cent." *(lit quote card, Chang's own words)*
9. Chang took the two apart. He inscribed the image of each obverse on Bitcoin, and had the physical coins melted and cast into one block, *CENTS Block Copper*.
10. Boris Groys describes two ways of archiving: "the object without an aura" and "the aura without an object." CENTS keeps both.
11. A cent held at one cent by the state became a Cent whose value and meaning are set, and set again, by the people who keep, read and exchange it.
12. Cent #1867, a great Cent. *(same ending: closing card, one turn)*
13. Find out more about the project on cents.gallery, and explore the traits on cents.vision. *(end card)*

## 5. Choices to check

- **No Hart.** "Every physical coin is two things in one body" and Hart's "token of authority" were the obvious carriers of this point. You cut that passage from the baseline at 22:31Z, so neither is used. Line 7 is my own short version of what the baseline still says in "The work".

## 6. Relational notes

- **Order.**
  - Line 8 comes before line 9: the gap in value, then Chang's act. The baseline supports that order, because the work's statement begins from these "discrepancies in value".
  - Nothing says the patinas, or any property of the coin, were a reason for anything Chang did. Line 6 is an observation about this coin.
- **Referents.**
  - "Compare it" (5) still follows a plural ("films"), so "it" is the Cent.
  - "The two" (9) is face and metal from line 8, which line 7 named as metal and stamp.
  - "both" (10) is Groys's two ways.
- **Registers.**
  - Metal and stamp appear four times, each doing something different. Line 6 is the object (this coin's surface), line 7 the definition, line 8 value (the gap), and lines 9 and 10 the form (CENTS taking them apart).
  - The film never says it twice in the same register.
- **Shape.**
  - The melt sits at the hinge, in the middle, not at the end.
  - The last idea is value being set again, then where to look next. The film does not end on the copper's loss.
