## 2. Script v0.8

1. Cent #1867. *(same opening: play button, "look closer" sticker, one 3DCENTS turn)*
2. Every physical coin joins two things: metal and stamp. *(concept card: "Metal and stamp")*
3. In a copper cent, the coin's face and its metal had come apart. Hoarding such coins, Chang said in 2017, was like "buying 1.7 cents of copper for one cent and it's still worth one cent." *(lit quote card, Chang's own words)*
4. Chang took the two apart. He inscribed the image of each obverse on Bitcoin, and had the physical coins melted and cast into one block, *CENTS Block Copper*. *(the image with its inscription number; the copper pour; the block)*
5. Boris Groys describes two ways of archiving: "the object without an aura" and "the aura without an object." CENTS keeps both. *(two-up: the block, then the image)*
6. In the image of Cent #1867, the copper's own chemistry spreads over the stamp of the state. *(back to the whole obverse, slow push-in)*
7. A dark band of Significant Obsidian crosses the upper left, over the motto and Lincoln's head.
8. Green patches of Significant Verdigris gather on his cheek, behind his head and at his collar.
9. A third trait came from outside: Contaminated, Alien Secretion. In the Traits Atlas, Alien Secretion names "unidentifiable translucent or greenish films".
10. Compare it to Cent #4421, also dated 1964 and close to mint state. *(dissolve)*
11. A cent held at one cent by the state became a Cent whose value and meaning are set, and set again, by the people who keep, read and exchange it. *(the coin alone, wide)*
12. Cent #1867, a great Cent. *(same ending: closing card, one turn)*
13. Find out more about the project on cents.gallery, and explore the traits on cents.vision. *(end card)*

## 3. Relational notes on the new order

- **Opening.**
  - The film now goes title, concept, then the origin in value (line 3, Chang's own words).
  - The origin is the first thing said about the work, as the baseline text gives it, so no property of the coin stands in front of it as a reason.
- **The melt (line 4)** now comes in the first half. It sits at a hinge, not at the end.
- **Groys to the traits.**
  - "The aura without an object" (5) is followed at once by the image (6).
  - Read that way, the trait zooms look at what the image keeps of a coin that is gone. The pictures support this: the two-up ends on the image, and the push-in continues from it.
- **The comparison (10)** now leads into the value line (11) instead of the turn. #4421 and #1867 had the same face value, and the value line says what each became as a Cent. The pictures carry that link; no extra words are needed.
- **Referents.**
  - "Compare it" (10) still follows "films", so "it" is the Cent.
  - "his cheek" (8) is Lincoln, named in line 7.
  - "the two" (4) is face and metal from line 3.
- **Ending.** The last idea is value being set again, then where to look next.
- **A small mismatch to know about.** The "look closer" sticker now comes before the discussion rather than right before the zooms. It still reads as an invitation, so I've kept it.
